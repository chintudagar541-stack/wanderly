# Reference style analysis (4 reference shorts, 59-65 s each, 480x854, 30 fps)

Method: 1 fps contact sheets of every second, 10 fps strips around transitions, frame-difference and Farneback optical-flow statistics for camera motion, histogram-correlation for scene-change timing.

## Numbers
| | v1 (buried bell) | v2 (fire button) | v3 (railroad) | v4 (dragon jar) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| distinct scene changes | 18 | 29 | 28 | 23 |
| average shot length | 3.1 s | 2.0 s | 2.3 s | 2.7 s |
| median shot length | 3.0 s | 1.4 s | 1.6 s | 1.8 s |
| hard cuts (frame diff spike) | 3 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| frames with camera push-in | 42% | 58% | 44% | 35% |
| frames with pull-out | 20% | 21% | 24% | 26% |
| mean zoom rate | 12%/s | 16%/s | 11%/s | 11%/s |
| mean pan rate | 3-7% of frame/s | | | |

Median frame-to-frame change is 5.6-8.9 grey levels: the picture is moving almost every frame. Almost no static shots.

## Camera
- Always moving. Default is a slow dolly push-in (about 10-15% closer per second), with pull-outs to reveal.
- Wide lens with strong perspective, low or high angles, foreground objects crossing the lens for depth.
- Camera flies through space: down a rope into the grave cross-section, through a window to the street, over a canyon edge.
- Big face close-ups at emotional beats; insert shots of props (certificate, bell, dragon head, clock, phone, FIRE button) to illustrate the exact word.
- Slight orbit/drift while a character talks so the background parallax stays alive.

## Transitions (no plain hard cuts)
1. Continuous camera flight where the next location is already in the same world.
2. Cross-dissolve of 0.3-0.4 s while the camera keeps moving and the grade shifts (day pink to night blue, cold street to warm interior).
3. Whip / zoom-blur through space (0.8 s) into the next set.
4. Match-action: papers flood a window, the camera passes through it into the exterior.
5. Anime speed lines + sweat drop + fast push-in at a shock beat, then a jerk to reveal.

## Characters
- Same ink-outline round head and stick limbs, but the acting is in the face: brows, eye shape (round pupils, wide eyes, happy arcs, half-lids), mouth (gritted teeth, open shout, smirk), sweat drops, steam puffs, face tint (purple with rage), head tilt, pointing hands.
- Expression changes about every 1-2 s, on the key word of the narration.

## Worlds
- Dense, fully built dioramas with real set dressing: clocks, desks, boxes, lanterns, bells, banners, snow, thrones. One dominant colour per location, local light pools (lantern glow, window light shafts), depth of field, bloom.

## Story telling
- A hook in the first second that shows the central object of the story.
- One subtitle word or short phrase per beat, white bold with dark outline, lower-middle of the frame.
- Each beat shows the thing being said, then moves on; escalation, reveal, payoff, and a closing image that echoes the opening.
