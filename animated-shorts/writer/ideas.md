# Idea bank (real stories, told in the FORMULA.md shape)

Each idea has a **hook** (first line), a **twist** (the hidden mechanism or payoff), a **world** (what to build), and a fact note.
✅ marks topics already made. Strike an idea through once it is made. 🇮🇳 marks an Indian story.

## Underdog laughed at, then proven right
1. **The jar that heard earthquakes** (China, 132 AD): the reference already did this one, so skip it.
2. **The doctor who said "wash your hands"** (Vienna, 1847).
   - Hook: "A doctor told other doctors to wash their hands, and they had him locked up."
   - Twist: the death rate in his ward dropped from about 18% to about 2%. Semmelweis died in an asylum, and germ theory proved him right years later.
   - World: a hospital ward, a lecture hall, a sink.
3. **The man who cut his own elevator rope** (New York, 1854).
   - Hook: "He climbed onto a platform high above a crowd and told a man to cut the rope."
   - Twist: Otis's safety brake catches it ("All safe, gentlemen!"). Skyscrapers become possible.
   - World: Crystal Palace expo, crowds, top hats.
4. **The schoolboy who froze hot ice cream** (Tanzania, 1963).
   - Hook: "A schoolboy said hot milk freezes faster than cold, and his whole class laughed."
   - Twist: Erasto Mpemba was right in his tests. A professor tested it, and they published together (the Mpemba effect is still debated, so say so).
   - World: a classroom, a cookery class, a freezer.
5. **The clock that beat the astronomers** (Britain, 1700s).
   - Hook: "A carpenter said he could find a ship's position with a clock, and the scientists laughed."
   - Twist: Harrison's sea watch kept time to seconds over a voyage. The board still dragged out paying him for years.
   - World: a ship deck in a storm, a workshop full of gears.
6. **The ice king** 🇮🇳 (Boston to Calcutta, 1833).
   - Hook: "A man shipped a mountain of ice four months across the ocean to India, and everyone said it would melt."
   - Twist: packed in sawdust, about two-thirds of it arrived. Calcutta built an ice house, and the British in India drank cold drinks.
   - World: a sailing ship, the Calcutta ghats, an ice house.
7. **The pigeon-guided missile** (USA, 1943).
   - Hook: "A scientist put three pigeons inside a bomb, and they steered it."
   - Twist: the pigeons pecked at a target image on a screen. It worked in tests; the military cancelled it anyway (Project Pigeon, B. F. Skinner).
   - World: a lab, a missile nose cone, a pigeon cockpit.

## Clever tricks and hidden mechanisms
8. **The horse that did maths** (Berlin, 1904).
   - Hook: "A horse could count, add, and tell the time. Scientists checked. No trick."
   - Twist: Clever Hans read tiny changes in the asker's face. When the asker didn't know the answer, neither did Hans.
   - World: a courtyard full of crowds, a blackboard.
9. **The chess robot with a man inside** (Vienna, 1770).
   - Hook: "A machine beat Napoleon at chess."
   - Twist: the Mechanical Turk hid a chess master in a sliding seat. It fooled people for about 80 years.
   - World: a palace, candles, the cabinet's inside.
10. **The dead man who fooled an army** (WWII, 1943).
    - Hook: "The British dressed a dead man as an officer and threw him into the sea."
    - Twist: Operation Mincemeat. Fake invasion plans in his briefcase sent German troops to the wrong place.
    - World: a submarine, a Spanish beach, an office.
11. **The pillar that won't rust** 🇮🇳 (Delhi, ~400 AD).
    - Hook: "An iron pillar has stood in the rain for sixteen hundred years and still hasn't rusted."
    - Twist: high phosphorus in the iron forms a thin protective layer (misawite). Ancient smiths' iron, done right.
    - World: the Qutub complex, tourists, monsoon.
12. **The Persian desert fridge** (Iran, ancient).
    - Hook: "In the middle of a desert, with no electricity, people made ice in summer."
    - Twist: a yakhchal. Shallow pools freeze at night under the open sky, the ice is stored in a giant domed pit, and wind towers keep it cool.
    - World: desert, a domed ice house, a caravan.
13. **Tipu's rockets** 🇮🇳 (Mysore, 1780s–90s).
    - Hook: "The British army got chased off a battlefield by flying iron tubes."
    - Twist: Mysore rockets had iron casings and went much further. Britain studied captured rockets and built the Congreve rocket.
    - World: a battlefield at dusk, a fort, a rocket workshop.
14. **The skyscraper with a secret** (New York, 1978).
    - Hook: "A student's question made an engineer sneak into a skyscraper at night to weld it."
    - Twist: Citicorp Center was weak against diagonal winds. The repairs were done quietly at night during hurricane season.
    - World: a city at night, welding sparks, an office.
15. **The window tax** (Britain, 1696–1851).
    - Hook: "In England, the government taxed sunlight."
    - Twist: houses were taxed by the number of windows, so people bricked them up. Dark rooms, illness, and bricked windows you can still see today.
    - World: a street of houses, bricklayers, a tax man.

## Rules that backfired
16. **The cobra bounty** 🇮🇳 (Delhi, British era; folklore, so hedge it).
    - Hook: "The government paid people for every dead cobra, and ended up with more cobras."
    - Twist: people bred cobras for the reward. When the bounty stopped, they released them ("the cobra effect").
    - World: a bazaar, an officer's desk, baskets.
17. **The rat tails of Hanoi** (1902, documented).
    - Hook: "The French paid one cent per rat tail. The rats kept running around without tails."
    - Twist: hunters cut off the tails, let the rats go to breed, and even farmed them.
    - World: sewers, an office with a pile of tails (cartoon), a colonial street.
18. **The Emu War** (Australia, 1932).
    - Hook: "Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to fight birds, and the birds won."
    - Twist: emus scattered, ran fast, and took hits. The army withdrew after weeks.
    - World: wheat fields, an army truck, a lot of emus.
19. **The ship that sank in 20 minutes** (Stockholm, 1628).
    - Hook: "The king's greatest warship sailed out in front of the whole city and sank before leaving the harbour."
    - Twist: in a stability test, 30 men ran across the deck and it rocked dangerously. No one dared tell the king. The Vasa was top-heavy.
    - World: the harbour, crowds, a ship deck.
20. **The beard tax** (Russia, 1698).
    - Hook: "In Russia, you needed a ticket to keep your beard."
    - Twist: Peter the Great wanted Russians to look European. Bearded men paid a tax and carried a beard token.
    - World: a palace, a barber, a town square.
21. **The copper coin disaster** 🇮🇳 (Tughlaq): the reference already made this, so skip it.
22. **The capital that walked** 🇮🇳 (Tughlaq, 1327): our Capital short (in progress). The script is in `capital_v2.txt`.

## Mysteries and strange history
23. **The dancing plague** (Strasbourg, 1518).
    - Hook: "One woman started dancing in the street and couldn't stop. Then four hundred people joined her."
    - Twist: the city hired musicians to "dance it out", and it got worse (likely mass stress and hysteria).
    - World: a medieval square, a stage, musicians.
24. **The molasses flood** (Boston, 1919).
    - Hook: "A wave of syrup taller than a house rolled through a city at 50 km/h."
    - Twist: a badly built tank burst. People said for decades the harbour smelled of molasses on hot days.
    - World: the harbour district, an elevated railway, the tank.
25. **Rent-a-pineapple** (Georgian Britain).
    - Hook: "In London, people rented a pineapple for the night, and didn't eat it."
    - Twist: pineapples were so rare they were status symbols. Hosts rented one to show off at parties.
    - World: a fancy dinner party, a fruit shop.
26. **The soldier who kept fighting for 29 years** (Philippines, 1945–74).
    - Hook: "A soldier kept fighting a war for twenty-nine years after it ended."
    - Twist: Hiroo Onoda refused to believe leaflets saying the war was over. His old commander had to fly in to order him to stop.
    - World: jungle, a hut, leaflets dropping.
27. **The stepwell air-conditioner** 🇮🇳 (Rajasthan/Gujarat).
    - Hook: "In the hottest place in India, people walked thirteen floors down to escape the heat."
    - Twist: stepwells (Chand Baori, Rani ki Vav) go deep to the water table. It is several degrees cooler at the bottom, so they were water stores and summer hangouts.
    - World: a giant geometric stepwell, women with pots.
28. **The dabbawala code** 🇮🇳 (Mumbai).
    - Hook: "Five thousand men deliver two hundred thousand lunches a day, mostly without computers, and almost never get one wrong."
    - Twist: a colour and number code painted on each tin, plus relay handoffs at train stations.
    - World: Mumbai local trains, bicycles, tiffin stacks.
29. **The sundial temple wheels** 🇮🇳 (Konark, 13th century).
    - Hook: "A temple in India has giant stone wheels, and they tell the time."
    - Twist: the shadow of a spoke on the wheel's carved beads works as a sundial.
    - World: the Sun temple chariot, horses, the sea.
30. **The penicillin that came too early** (France, 1897).
    - Hook: "A young army doctor discovered penicillin thirty years before Fleming. Nobody read his paper."
    - Twist: Ernest Duchesne noticed Arab stable boys kept saddles where mould grew. His thesis was ignored, and he died young.
    - World: stables, a lab, a lecture hall.

## How to pick
- Prefer: underdog + mockery + mechanism (2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 14, 19). These match the 10M-view pattern closest.
- 🇮🇳 topics (6, 11, 13, 16, 27, 28, 29) are good for the Indian audience.
- Check the facts before writing. Write any legend as "one story says".
