The Sultan of Delhi ordered his whole capital to pack up and move. Not the palace. Everyone.
You see, it's thirteen twenty seven, and Muhammad bin Tughlaq has a problem. Delhi is right where the Mongols keep showing up, and the south of his empire is months away.
So he points at a map. Devagiri. Dead centre. He renames it Daulatabad, the city of fortune, and announces, "Everybody goes!"
Nobles. Soldiers. Barbers. Grandmas. The guy who sells samosas.
"All of us?" "All of you!"
And to be fair, he tries to make it nice. A royal road. Shady trees. Rest houses. Free food and water.
Still, it's over a thousand kilometres. On foot. In summer.
People start dropping along the road.
Back in Delhi, the streets go silent. One traveller claims the soldiers found only two men left, and one was blind. They dragged him all the way to Daulatabad.
Only his leg arrived.
And Daulatabad? Gorgeous. A fortress on a giant rock. Except now rebels pop up in the north, and the Sultan is forty days away.
So a few years later, he has a brand new idea.
"Everybody! Back to Delhi!"
Same road. Same heat. Same forty days. Backwards.
Historians call him the wise fool.
