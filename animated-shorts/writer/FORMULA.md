# Story-short formula (from studying 10M-view reference shorts)

These notes come from four reference shorts the owner sent on 2026-11-07:
- **Bell coffin**: a man is buried with a bell.
- **Japan's "nothing room"**: the room workers are sent to so they quit.
- **Railroad gaps**: rails laid with gaps every 39 ft.
- **Earthquake jar**: the dragon jar that detects earthquakes.

Each one is about 60–65 s long, about 225–245 words, and about **3.7 words per second**.

## Why they get 10M views
1. **The hook is one weird, visual sentence, and it does not explain itself.**
   - Examples: "They buried a man alive with a bell tied to his finger." "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth and told the Emperor: if this ever falls out, run!"
   - A strange action plus a missing reason makes a curiosity gap in the first 3 s.
2. **The answer is held back until the last 15 s.** The viewer has to stay to find out *why*. That keeps watch time near 100%, and the algorithm pushes it.
3. **There is a tiny human story, not a lecture.**
   - One nobody (an engineer, an inventor, a worker, a wife) faces a laughing or bossy crowd (a boss, the Emperor, neighbours, the court).
   - Facts get delivered *through* what happens to them.
4. **Mockery escalates**: "The whole court burst out laughing... Now they laughed even harder."
   - It makes the viewer side with the underdog and wait for the payback.
5. **The twist at ~60% is a single word or short line**: "Wait. *Practiced?*" / "Then one Monday... he's gone."
6. **The payoff is a real mechanism** (physics, law, a trick, money), explained visually in 2–3 lines.
7. **The punchline is dark or ironic, and the video ends hard**: "...and the house was all hers." Then cut.
   - There is no moral, no "subscribe", and no outro. The abrupt end makes people rewatch (loops).
8. **The topic is universal**: work, money, family, rulers, a clever trick. Anyone in any country gets it.

## Script beat sheet (aim 215–240 words, 58–65 s)
| time | beat | how |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | **HOOK** | One sentence, ≤ 18 words. A weird concrete act or claim. No date or name first. |
| 3–12 s | **"You see,"** | Context in 1–2 sentences: where, when, what the stakes are. |
| 12–25 s | **The nobody vs the crowd** | Introduce the protagonist plus the authority that doubts or mocks them. Use **quoted dialogue** ("You go check!" "No, YOU go check!"). |
| 25–38 s | **Escalation** | 2–3 short steps with time markers: "Weeks later...", "By July...", "Then one night...". |
| 38–42 s | **TWIST** | One word or a very short line. "Wait." / "Then he's gone." |
| 42–55 s | **The mechanism** | Explain the hidden *why* simply ("Inside the jar hung a pendulum..."). |
| 55–62 s | **PUNCHLINE** | A short ironic or dark last line. Stop right there. |

## Line rules
- Write in present tense, and talk to the viewer ("Imagine your boss...", "And you're thinking...").
- Use short sentences, mostly 4–12 words. Fragments are fine: "On foot. In summer."
- Use playful asides: "like he's on vacation", "the world's most expensive toy".
- Use at least 2 quoted lines of dialogue that a character says, with funny reactions.
- Use concrete numbers and objects (39 feet, a finger-wide gap, three short rings, one long).
- Use connective rhythm words: "You see," "Except", "Then", "Meanwhile", "Still,", "So". Never "In conclusion".
- Avoid lists of dates, encyclopedia tone, and moralising. Hedge folklore with "one story says".
- The last line is ≤ 10 words.

## Picture rules learned (in addition to STYLE.md)
- **Angle change every ~1 s, cut on words.** Many changes are fast whip or dissolve moves, not plain hard cuts.
- **Low, wide-lens camera** near the ground (rails, gravel, floor). Characters **lean into the lens** for big face close-ups.
- **Reaction gags on the face**: the face turns purple with anger, steam puffs, sweat drops, eyes pop wide, a nightcap.
- **Insert close-ups of key props** with readable text: a death certificate, a phone inbox "(0)", a calendar "NOV FRI 25", a newspaper headline.
- **Cutaway or cross-section diagrams** for the mechanism: a grave cross-section with a rope, the pendulum inside the jar, a rail gap shrinking with a coin.
- **Strong colour mood per beat**: blue night with a warm lamp; a golden sunset for the ending; a saturated sky.
- **Dense, textured sets**:
  - Patterned wallpaper, quilts, rugs, bunting, crowds, vendors.
  - Big establishing "crowd" shots: an army of hundreds, a whole street on their porches.
- The climax ends on the biggest visual (an explosion, rubble, a sunrise), then cut.
