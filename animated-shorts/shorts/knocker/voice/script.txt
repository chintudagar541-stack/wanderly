Before alarm clocks, whole towns paid one man to wake them up.
He was called a knocker upper.
Every morning, he tapped on your window with a long pole, and he didn't leave until you answered.
And this was a real job.
In eighteen hundreds Britain, factory workers paid them a few pennies a week.
Some didn't even use a pole.
Mary Smith of London shot dried peas at your window through a peashooter.
But that raised one obvious question.
Who woke up the knocker upper?
Most of them simply never slept at night.
They worked until sunrise and went to bed when everyone else got up.
Then cheap alarm clocks arrived, and the job vanished almost overnight.
So the next time your alarm goes off, just be glad it isn't a man with a stick.
