In nineteen twenty-five, a man arrived in Paris with a fake name, and a very big idea.
The newspapers said the Eiffel Tower was rusting, and repairing it was costing the city a fortune.
Victor Lustig smiled.
He forged government letters, and invited six scrap metal dealers to a secret meeting, at one of the finest hotels in Paris.
There, he told them the city could no longer afford the tower.
It would be torn down, and sold as scrap. Seven thousand tons of iron.
But it had to stay secret, or Paris would riot.
He drove the dealers to the tower in a limousine.
And he picked one man. André Poisson, a dealer desperate to feel important.
But Poisson's wife was suspicious.
So Lustig made a confession. He was a poorly paid official, and he needed a little bribe.
That convinced them. Only a real official would ask for a bribe.
Poisson paid for the tower, and the bribe.
Lustig took the next train to Vienna, with a suitcase full of cash.
And Poisson was too embarrassed to tell the police.
So one month later, Lustig came back, and sold the Eiffel Tower again.
This time, the buyer went to the police. But Lustig had already vanished.
The tower was never for sale.
But Victor Lustig sold it.
Twice.
