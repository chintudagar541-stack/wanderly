Imagine celebrating the same festival, but for completely different reasons.
Welcome to India, where even Diwali has multiple storylines.
Let's start with Ayodhya.
Around twenty five hundred years ago... well, according to the Ramayana tradition, Lord Rama returned home after fourteen years of exile, and the people welcomed him by lighting thousands of diyas.
Basically, the biggest homecoming party ever.
Now travel to Amritsar.
Here, the same day has another important meaning.
It marks Bandi Chhor Divas, when Guru Hargobind Ji was released from imprisonment along with fifty two other kings.
And the Golden Temple lights up like someone just turned on the entire universe.
Next stop, Kolkata.
Forget the usual Lakshmi Puja for a moment.
Here, Diwali night is famous for Kali Puja.
People worship Goddess Kali, the powerful goddess associated with protection and the destruction of evil.
And trust me, the decorations are anything but ordinary.
Now let's fly to Goa.
Here, the celebrations begin with a giant demon named Narakasura.
People build enormous effigies of him and burn them before sunrise to celebrate Lord Krishna's victory over the demon.
Imagine waking up early just to watch a demon get evicted.
And finally, Varanasi.
Here, there's another spectacular celebration called Dev Deepawali.
But plot twist!
It's not on the main Diwali night.
It happens around fifteen days later, when the ghats of the Ganges are decorated with countless glowing diyas.
It's believed that the gods themselves come down to celebrate.
So why does India celebrate Diwali in so many different ways?
Because different regions remember different legends, victories, and traditions.
One festival.
Different stories.
And somehow, everyone agrees on one thing.
More lights, more sweets, and absolutely no shortage of celebrations.
