Imagine your king wakes up one morning and says, pack your bags. All of you.
It's thirteen twenty seven. Sultan Muhammad bin Tughlaq rules India from Delhi. Smart man. Reads philosophy. Writes poetry.
But he has a tiny problem. Delhi is too close to the Mongols, and way too far from the south.
So he points at a map. Devagiri. Right in the middle. Perfect! He renames it Daulatabad. The city of wealth.
Now, a normal king would just move his office.
Not Tughlaq. He moves everyone.
Nobles. Soldiers. Barbers. Grandmas. The guy who sells samosas. Everyone!
To be fair, he builds a royal road. Shady trees. Rest houses every two miles. Free water.
Still, it's eleven hundred kilometres. On foot. In summer.
People get sick. Many never make it.
Back in Delhi? Silence. One story says only a blind man was left, and even he got dragged along.
And Daulatabad? Great city. Terrible location. Rebels pop up in the north, and the Sultan is forty days away.
So a few years later, he makes a brand new announcement.
Pack your bags. Again.
Same road. Same heat. Same forty days. Backwards.
Historians call him the wise fool.
Brilliant ideas. Terrible timing.
